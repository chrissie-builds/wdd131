Beyond the web

There’s more than one way
to be a spider.

Some build intricate traps. Others stalk, leap or wait in a sheltered retreat. Spiders are arachnids, alongside scorpions, ticks and mites. Unlike insects, they have eight legs and no antennae.

Salticidae · Active hunter

Jumping spiders

Large forward-facing eyes help these compact spiders judge distance before a leap. A silk safety line helps them return if a jump goes wrong.

Habitat
Leaves, walls and garden vegetation.
Diet
Small insects and other arthropods.

Araneidae · Web builder

Orb-weavers

An orb web is a hunting tool. Threads transmit vibrations that help its owner locate struggling prey. Web shape and building routines vary among species.

Habitat
Gardens, woodland and spaces between plants.
Diet
Flying insects caught in silk.

Lycosidae · Active hunter

Wolf spiders

These ground hunters search for prey instead of relying on an aerial capture web. Females carry an egg sac attached to their spinnerets and later carry young on their backs.

Habitat
Ground-level vegetation, leaf litter and burrows.
Diet
Insects and other small invertebrates.
